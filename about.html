Why Midly exists

Contracts don't die in legal review. They die in the gap.

Most deal failures don't happen because the contract was bad. They happen in the silence between “yes” on the call and the moment the agreement is signed. Counterparty paper sits in someone's inbox for three weeks. A redline gets missed on a 40-page MSA. An associate forwards the wrong version. The deal slides past quarter-end and dies.

The tools that exist for this problem were built on the wrong side of the table. Legacy CLM platforms were designed for in-house legal departments: built around procurement workflows, approval matrices, and contract lifecycle stages that mean something to a 50-person legal team. They don't mean much to an outside counsel attorney reviewing three counterparty papers before lunch, or to the one lawyer who is the entire legal function at a growing company.

Contracts shouldn't be where deals die. They should be where deals get done.

Midly was built for the people actually doing the work. The attorney reviewing the redline. The one lawyer holding the line for a whole company. The client who needs to see what's happening with their own paperwork. We move the AI, the playbook, and the workflow surface to where the contract is actually being moved, not to where someone in legal will eventually file a copy. (See how the workflow runs end-to-end.)

Midly treats counterparty paper as the main event, not the exception. Forward or upload a redlined .docx from the other side and every tracked change is parsed and graded against your playbook in minutes rather than hours. Outside counsel get a client portal and billable-time tracking in the same place as the review. And every insight is checked against the clause it cites, so each finding points to language you can verify yourself.

What we believe

Three things we won't compromise on.

Every product decision we make runs through these.

01
AI grounded in the document.
Every insight cites the exact clause it came from. If the model can't ground an answer in the source, it shouldn't offer one. No hallucinated case law. No invented contract history.
02
Built for the user, not the buyer.
The person opening the contract is the one we're designing for, not the committee that bought the seat license. If the lawyer doing the work will not open it, nothing else about the tool matters.
03
Counterparty paper is a first-class object.
The hardest review is the one you didn't draft. Every other CLM treats counterparty paper as an exception. We treat it as the default, because for most teams, it is.
