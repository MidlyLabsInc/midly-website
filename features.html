Features

What Midly does.

Follow one contract from the moment the other side's draft lands in your inbox to the renewal alert two years later. Every line below is a specific you can check, not a claim about how it feels.

One. It arrives

Their paper, opened without being flattened.

Most of what you review is somebody else's document, sent back with changes in it. Getting it into a tool is usually where the changes die.

Your workspace has its own address
Forward the other side's draft from wherever it landed and it is open and read before you would have finished saving it to a folder. Nothing to upload.
Every workspace is issued an intake address at in.midly.ai. Their tracked changes arrive intact, not flattened into plain text.
Tracked changes stay tracked changes
Every insertion and deletion the other side sent is a real tracked change you accept or reject where it sits. No downloading to Word, marking up there, and uploading it back.
Accept and reject happen in the document, with the author of each change read from the file rather than guessed.
Two. You read it

A hundred changes come back and two of them move the risk.

The reading is the product. Everything else on this page exists so you never have to leave the document to finish the job.

Graded, not listed
Every clause is graded against your own playbook and anchored to the exact language it is talking about, so you can check each finding against your own document instead of taking it on trust.
Findings are anchored to the clause they came from. A finding you cannot trace to language is a finding you should not act on.
Ask it anything, about that document
Highlight a clause you do not like the look of and ask what it actually does. No hallucinated case law and no invented citations.
Every factual claim carries a verbatim quote from your document. Where there is no passage to quote, the answer is "This information is not stated in the document."
Compare two versions
Put the version you sent beside the version that came back and see what moved, separated into the changes that carry legal weight and the ones that do not.
Reports material and legal deviations between two drafts, rather than a character diff that marks every comma.
It remembers what you argued
Your playbook, your standing instructions for a client, and the positions you took in the last round all carry into the next review. The fiftieth time you argue a clause does not start where the first one did.
Positions carry across rounds, so the same clause is not re-litigated from nothing each time the draft comes back.
Three. The round after

What happened to the changes you sent.

Their markup is easy to find. It is in the document, in colour, with their name on it. What happened to yours is the part nobody can see.

Your undone changes, named
A change they accepted becomes plain text that looks like every clause around it. A change they rejected is simply gone, with nothing left to point at. So the carve-out you negotiated for can leave the next draft without a word.
Midly compares the two versions and reports what was undone first, ahead of the findings from their new markup. Anything under twelve characters is left out, because a word like "and" says nothing about whether a change survived.
Edits written, not just flagged
Midly does not stop at telling you the cap is wrong. Ask Midly drafts the replacement language with the reason beside it, and you decide what goes into the document.
Proposed language lands as a tracked change in your name, with the argument attached, ready to send.
Four. It gets signed

Signature is not the finish line.

It is the start of the part you have to live with. Most software stops here, which is why most agreements go in a drawer and come out only when something has gone wrong.

Signing in the order you set
A contract with two signers goes out one signer at a time, each on their own link, each signing only their own side of the execution block. A signer whose turn has not come sees a waiting screen rather than a live pad.
Signing order is set per document. A signer can decline, which stops the chain and tells you, with their reason if they gave one.
The agreement, read back to you
The moment it is executed, Midly reads it and holds what is actually in it, so the terms you are living under are a question you can ask rather than a document you have to go and find.
Holds the type, the effective and expiry dates, the governing law, the auto-renewal and its notice period, termination for convenience, the payment terms, the liability cap, whether indemnities sit outside it, and who owns the work.
The renewal comes and finds you
Auto-renewal and the notice period are pulled out as dates rather than left as a sentence in clause 14.
Midly emails you when the window to get out opens, while there is still time to use it.
Your client gets the version they can read
One button sends them a plain summary of what they agreed to, in terms rather than in analysis.
Your playbook analysis stays with you. What your house position says about their deal is your work, not theirs.
Five. Across every client

How your markups actually land.

Round three opens and the question is how hard to push with this counterparty. The answer is already in the signed agreements for that client. It has just never been counted.

Per client, per clause
Not one blended figure across every negotiation you have ever run. Each client's own record, broken down by the kind of clause you were arguing about.
Eighteen named clause families, from limitation of liability and indemnity to insurance, IP and assignment. Three outcomes: accepted as proposed, accepted on reworded terms, refused.
Signed paper only, and it says when it does not know
A change marked accepted mid negotiation can be reopened next round, so it only counts once the paper is signed. And a rate off one or two answers is noise, so you see the counts until there are enough of them.
Counts executed agreements only. A percentage appears once three markups in a family have been answered. A new client shows nothing until there is history to read.
